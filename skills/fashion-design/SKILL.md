# Fashion Design Knowledge

## Garment Construction Basics

- Always cut with grainline parallel to fabric selvage unless bias is specified.
- Use appropriate seam allowances: 1/2" (1.25cm) standard, 1/4" (0.6cm) for knits, 5/8" (1.5cm) for curved seams.
- Press seams open or to one side as specified; pressing is part of construction.
- Finish raw edges (serge, zigzag, or bind) to prevent fraying.
- Baste before final stitching on complex seams.

## Standard Body Measurements (inches)

Women base (M): Bust 36, Waist 29, Hip 39, Shoulder 15, Inseam 31, Height 66.
Men base (M): Chest 38, Waist 32, Hip 38, Shoulder 17.5, Inseam 32, Height 69.

Common sizes: XS, S, M, L, XL, XXL. Grade increments typically 2" (5cm) for circumference, 0.5" (1.25cm) for length/shoulder.

## Fabric Properties

- Woven: stable, less stretch; use for structured garments. Grain matters.
- Knit: stretch and recovery; use ballpoint needles; avoid over-pressing.
- Denim: heavy twill; pre-wash to prevent shrinkage.
- Silk: delicate; use fine needles, low heat.
- Wool: can felt; dry clean; press with cloth.

## Grading Rules

- Grade circumference measurements by 2" per size step.
- Grade length by 0.5"-1" per size step.
- Maintain proportional balance; check armhole and neckline.
- Base size is usually M or size 8/10.

## Tech Pack Standards

A tech pack includes:
1. Flat sketch (front/back) with points of measure.
2. Measurement chart with tolerance (+/- 0.25").
3. Construction details and stitch types.
4. Materials: fabric, lining, trims, thread, labels.
5. Grading rules.
6. Labeling and packaging specs.
7. Colorways.

## Bill of Materials

List every component with quantity, unit, supplier, and cost. Include fabric, lining, thread, trims, hardware, labels, packaging.

## Costing

Per-unit cost = fabric + labor + trims + overhead (typically 10%). Suggested retail = 2.5-3x cost.

## Collection Planning

A balanced collection: 40% tops, 30% bottoms, 20% dresses, 10% outerwear. Use 5-color palette: 2 hero, 2 supporting, 1 neutral.
